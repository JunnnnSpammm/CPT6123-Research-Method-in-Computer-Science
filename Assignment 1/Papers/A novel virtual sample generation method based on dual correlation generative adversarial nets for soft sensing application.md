### A novel virtual sample generation method based on dual correlation generative adversarial nets for soft sensing application

---

### Introduction and Motivation

* In industrial production, data-driven soft sensors often suffer from poor prediction performance due to the "big data, small sample" problem.


* While Virtual Sample Generation (VSG) techniques exist to expand datasets, existing methods (like standard GANs) primarily focus on sample inputs, neglecting the outputs and the vital mapping relationship between them.


* To resolve this, the authors introduce a Dual Correlation Generative Adversarial Nets-based Virtual Sample Generation method (DCRGAN-VSG).



### The DCRGAN-VSG Methodology

* The architecture utilizes a Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) to generate process variables and an improved Conditional GAN (CGAN) to generate quality variables.


* To prevent sample-label mismatches, Mean Square Error (MSE) is added to the CGAN's value function, creating an RC-CGAN that maintains the original input-output mapping.


* The unified DCRGAN relies on two generators and two discriminators sharing a latent space to output paired inputs and outputs directly.


* Instead of retaining all generated data, the Pielou index is used to selectively filter virtual samples; only samples that fall into sparse density intervals and increase the overall uniformity of the dataset are kept.



### Benchmark Simulation Results

* Tested on a 3-dimensional standard function, the standalone RC-CGAN reduced the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) test error by nearly 40% compared to a baseline CGAN.


* When evaluating the full DCRGAN-VSG against competing methods (VAE, WGAN-GP, DDPM, TTD), the proposed model achieved the lowest Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 0.0696 and highest $R^{2}$ fit at an optimal generation of 420 virtual samples.


* The study highlights that generating too many virtual samples (e.g., beyond 500) can over-saturate the spatial characteristics of the real samples, causing the prediction error to rise again.



### Industrial Case Study and Conclusions

* The method was validated on a real-world Purified Terephthalic Acid (PTA) industrial dataset comprising 17 process variables used to predict acetic acid conductivity.


* Adding virtual samples generated by DCRGAN-VSG improved the prediction accuracy of the Light-GBM soft sensor by nearly 40%, proving vastly superior to methods like Tree-based Trend Diffusion (TTD) that ignore variable correlations.


* The paper concludes that DCRGAN-VSG successfully fills data gaps without destroying original distributions, with future research slated to focus on rapidly finding the exact optimal number of virtual samples to generate.
